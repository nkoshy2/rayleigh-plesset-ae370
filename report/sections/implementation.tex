\section{Correct implementation}\label{sec:implementation}

We wrote both integrators ourselves in Python with NumPy; no library time
integrator is called anywhere. The code separates the method from the problem:
\texttt{src/rp/integrators.py} holds RK4, DP5 with a fixed step, and adaptive
DP5 (Algorithms~\ref{alg:rk4} and~\ref{alg:dp5}), each taking a generic
right-hand side $\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y})$ and counting its evaluations;
\texttt{src/rp/model.py} holds Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhs}, its Jacobian, the three test
cases and the reference solutions; and every figure below is produced by one
script in \texttt{experiments/}. A suite of 24 unit tests (\texttt{pytest})
checks the Butcher tableaus against the order conditions of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:order} (and the nine fifth-order conditions for DP5), the
stability polynomials, the nondimensionalization against the SI form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rp}, and each verification result reported here.

The rubric asks for two things: that the method converges at the expected rate,
and that it converges to the solution of the right equations. A convergence
study against a fine-step reference shows only the first, since a wrong term in
$\mathbf{f}$ converges just as cleanly. We therefore check the answer against
three things we know independently of our solver, one per test case
(Section~\ref{sec:impl-verify}), before measuring rates on the full problem
(Section~\ref{sec:impl-rate}).

\subsection{Converging to the right answer}\label{sec:impl-verify}

\paragraph{Case A: exact Rayleigh collapse.} For the empty cavity, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rayleigh-t}
gives the exact time to reach any radius. We evaluate its integral by
Gauss--Legendre quadrature after the substitution $x=1-s^2$, which removes the
endpoint singularity; this reproduces $\tilde t_c=0.9146813565$ to $10^{-14}$
and is independent of any time stepping. Figure~\ref{fig:caseA}(a) integrates
to the exact time $\tilde t^*=0.825457$ at which $\tilde R=0.5$ and plots the
error in $\tilde R(\tilde t^*)$. Both fixed-step methods converge to the exact
value, with observed orders $3.99$ (RK4) and $4.97$ (DP5) from the two finest
steps. Deeper into the collapse the derivatives of $\mathbf{f}$ grow and the
asymptotic range starts at smaller $h$, as Section~\ref{sec:method-error}
anticipates: at $\tilde R=0.2$ the same runs give orders $3.93$ and $4.3$.
Adaptive DP5 has no such difficulty. Run with $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-12}$ until
$\tilde R=10^{-3}$, where $|\tilde R'|\approx2.6\times10^{4}$, it reaches that
radius at a time that differs from the exact one by $7\times10^{-14}$, and the
state there satisfies the first integral Eq.~\eqref{eq:rayleigh-energy} to
$10^{-8}$.

\paragraph{Case B: energy conservation.} With $\mu=0$, the energy of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy} is exactly conserved, so its drift measures how well we
solve the actual equations, gas and surface-tension terms included. For
$\Pi=10$ over $0\le\tilde t\le8$ (about four collapse and rebound cycles), the
maximum relative drift falls from $2.6\times10^{-3}$ to $4.6\times10^{-10}$ as
RK4 is refined from 400 to 12{,}800 steps, a factor of about $2^{4.3}$ per
halving, and adaptive DP5 reaches $3\times10^{-12}$ at $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-12}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:caseB}(b)).

\paragraph{Case C: linear response.} At $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ the forced response
is linear to $O(\varepsilon)$, so the steady amplitude from the full nonlinear
equation must match the damped, driven oscillator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:linear}. For
$R_0=10\ \mu$m we integrate for 700 time units (about 13 damping times) and
measure the amplitude over the next 40 periods. Across
$0.5\le\omega/\omega_0\le2$, including the resonance peak where the amplitude
is twelve times the static response, the numerical and linear amplitudes agree
to better than $1.4\times10^{-4}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:caseC}), and to better than
$5\times10^{-5}$ at all but the highest frequency. The run also reproduces the
values from the premise: $\omega_0 t_R=2.161$, $f_0=342.5$\,kHz and $Q=53.6$.

\subsection{Convergence rate on the full problem}\label{sec:impl-rate}

Figure~\ref{fig:caseB}(a) uses the baseline Case B ($R_0=100\ \mu$m, $\Pi=5$,
all physics on) and measures the error in $\tilde R$ at $\tilde t=3$, after the
first collapse and into the rebound, against an adaptive DP5 reference at
$\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-13}$. The observed orders are $3.96$ for RK4 and $4.97$ for
fixed-step DP5, matching $p=4$ and $p=5$. The curves are irregular at the
coarsest steps, where a step is comparable to the time spent near $R_{\min}$;
this is the pre-asymptotic regime of Section~\ref{sec:method-error}.

\subsection{Stability along collapse trajectories}\label{sec:impl-stability}

Section~\ref{sec:method-why} argued from the linearization about equilibrium
that the problem is not stiff. Figure~\ref{fig:stability} checks this where it
matters, during violent motion. At every accepted DP5 step
($\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-8}$) of a Case B collapse at $\Pi=20$ and of 20 forcing
periods of Case C at $\varepsilon=0.3$, we compute the eigenvalues $\lambda$ of
the analytic Jacobian of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhs} and plot $h\lambda$. The largest
value is $|h\lambda|=0.16$, against stability limits of $2.79$ (RK4, real
axis), $2.83$ (RK4, imaginary axis), $3.31$ (DP5, real axis) and $1.00$ (DP5,
imaginary axis). The step is therefore always set by accuracy. Panel (b) also
shows how the controller works: $|h\lambda|$ stays near $0.1$ throughout, so
the step shrinks in proportion to the local time scale $1/|\lambda|$. Some
eigenvalues have positive real part during the collapse. That is the physical
growth of the collapsing motion, which an accurate method has to follow, not a
numerical instability.

\subsection{Choosing the simulation parameters}\label{sec:impl-params}

Q1 needs $R_{\min}$, the peak inward wall speed and the peak gas pressure. The
last is the most sensitive, because $p_{g,\max}\propto R_{\min}^{-3\kappa}$
amplifies relative errors by about $4.2$. Figure~\ref{fig:tolerance} measures
all three against a reference at $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-13}$ for the pressure
ratios we will sweep. To keep every quantity within $10^{-6}$ of the reference,
far below what a plot can show, $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-7}$ is enough for all
$\Pi$. We use $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-8}$ and $\mathrm{atol}=10^{-10}$ for a
margin of ten: the worst error is then $1.3\times10^{-7}$ (peak gas pressure at
$\Pi=20$), and a run to the first collapse costs only 270 to 510 evaluations of
$\mathbf{f}$. The extrema are located by the event search of
Section~\ref{sec:method-singular}, so they inherit this accuracy instead of
being limited by where the steps happen to fall.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_tolerance.pdf}
  \caption{Relative error of the Q1 quantities versus DP5 tolerance for Case B
  at $R_0=100\ \mu$m, measured against $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-13}$. Dashed line:
  the $10^{-6}$ accuracy target.}
  \label{fig:tolerance}

Adaptivity pays off most in the strongest collapses. For $\Pi=20$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stepsize}) the accepted step varies by a factor of 166, from
$6.8\times10^{-2}$ to $4.1\times10^{-4}$ (in units of $t_R$), shrinking sharply
in the brief interval around each minimum. DP5 reaches an error of
$9\times10^{-9}$ at $\tilde t=3$ with 1{,}508 evaluations (221 accepted and 30
rejected steps). Fixed-step RK4 needs about 5{,}500 steps, or 21{,}900
evaluations, for the same error, so the adaptive method is about 15 times
cheaper here, and the gap widens as $\Pi$ grows. Hence we use fixed-step RK4
only for the convergence studies above, and adaptive DP5 for every result in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

One finding matters for the results: at $\Pi=20$ the peak wall Mach number is
$|\dot R|_{\max}/c=0.14$, above the cutoff of $0.1$ set in
Section~\ref{sec:ivp}. The upper end of our planned $\Pi$ range is therefore
outside the model's validity, and we will mark it as such in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.
